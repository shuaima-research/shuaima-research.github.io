Research Papers and Writing Samples

Hazard or Amenity? Flood Risk, Waterway Proximity, and Neighborhood Inequality 2025–Present
Sole-authored PhD writing sample; empirical study of Pierce County, Washington

  • Examines how flood risk, waterway proximity, and neighborhood inequality are jointly reflected in residential prices, using 47,763 single-family sales (2021–2025) linked to parcel, FEMA flood-risk, and neighborhood data; recasts whether flood risk is discounted into how hazard, amenity, and neighborhood advantage occupy the same ground.
  • Combines hedonic regression (tract-clustered standard errors) with spatial analysis (Moran’s I, LISA), a flood–income–waterway typology, and machine-learning checks, finding no reliable flood-risk discount because hazard and waterfront amenity are too co-located for price alone to separate.
  • Shows that where hazard and amenity share the same parcels, prices cannot reveal who bears flood risk, so value-oriented flood-mitigation investment tends to flow to already-advantaged neighborhoods.

Strategic Management’s Foundational Silence 2025–Present
First-author manuscript in revision, coauthored with Rubina Mahsud and Greg Prussia; developed from a 2020 Strategic Management Society conference proposal

Selected Research and Analytical Projects

Pro Forma Urbanism: Financial Feasibility Modeling and Land Value Capture in Shenzhen Urban Renewal 2025
Sole-authored research brief

  • Reverse-engineered a state-owned developer’s 45-slide feasibility deck, rebuilding its static and dynamic discounted-cash-flow pro forma as reproducible spreadsheets and validating them against the source’s reported outputs (IRR 27.45 percent; NPV ≈ ¥2.44 billion), to show how the model prices land and decides what gets built.
  • Reconstructed Shenzhen’s FAR-graduated, tenure-contingent land-pricing rules to trace how density entitlements convert into a payable land premium, finding that roughly 86 percent of the premium falls on the new, market-priced density the plan creates—floor-area ratio functioning as a value-capture instrument.

Empirical Analysis of Guangzhou Residential Rental Markets 2021
Sole-authored hedonic study

  • Collected and cleaned 3,000 Guangzhou rental listings and built a hedonic log-rent model with robust standard errors and multicollinearity diagnostics, finding floor area to be the dominant structural correlate of rent (elasticity ≈ 0.72).
  • Estimated conditional district rent differentials to characterize the spatial structure of the rental market, finding that central, employment- and transit-rich districts retain substantial premiums after dwelling attributes are controlled (approximately 51 percent in Tianhe, 42 percent in Yuexiu, and 27 percent in Haizhu relative to Baiyun), and interpreting these as capitalization patterns rather than causal effects given the data’s measurement limits.

Operationalizing Highest and Best Use: A GDV-Oriented Integer-Programming Approach to Product-Mix Optimization for Business-Park Development 2021
Initiated and led an internal real-estate analytics study

  • Operationalized Highest and Best Use as an integer linear program that optimizes a business park’s product mix under floor-area-ratio and plot-coverage constraints, solved to optimality in seconds with open-source tools.
  • Used sensitivity analysis to quantify the GDV cost of prioritizing market absorption over pure value maximization, turning a single-objective optimum into a risk-aware product-mix recommendation.

Engineering Controlled Flexibility: New-type Industrial Land (M0) and the Governance of Innovation Space 2021
Sole-authored comparative policy analysis

  • Coded industrial land-use policy across seven Pearl River Delta cities along six regulatory dimensions to theorize Shenzhen’s M0 land category as “controlled flexibility” enforced through conditional property rights.
  • Triangulated policy texts with 2021 market evidence to show how flexible industrial zoning drifts toward saleable office-like product, requiring continual loophole-closing and a spatial block-line counterweight.

Land Use as a Hydrological Variable: A Site-Conditioned Framework for Low-Impact Development 2011–2017
Sole-authored retrospective multi-case study

  • Synthesized six sponge-city/LID commissions from watershed to CBD-plot scale into a comparative case series, arguing that land use is a first-order determinant of stormwater design and deriving five site-conditioned propositions linking terrain, soil, groundwater, and density to treatment-train configuration.
  • Conducted the underlying hydrological modeling, including SWMM simulation of lake storage and ponding risk under design storms and runoff calculations showing land conversion doubling peak flow on a low-permeability site, to ground a land-use-conditioned LID design framework.

Auditing Carbon at the Scale of the Urban System: A Spatial Planning Emissions Model for Jiangsu Province 2011
